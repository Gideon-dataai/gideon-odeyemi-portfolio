Emergency Department Throughput Analysis

An end-to-end operational case study using 4,000 synthetic emergency department visits at the fictional Cedarbridge General Hospital (January–October 2026).

The business problem

Emergency departments balance unpredictable arrivals, varying urgency and inpatient bed constraints. This fictional hospital scenario examines when patient flow slows, how waits differ across patient groups and how admission boarding affects throughput.

Questions explored

Demand and flow

Which days and time bands carry the most visits? How do arrival modes and acuity levels differ?

Waiting and walk-outs

How often are assumed wait targets met, and where do long waits and patients leaving without being seen coincide?

Admissions and boarding

How do admission patterns, boarding time and length of stay vary across groups?

Operational decisions

Which findings could guide staffing discussions, fast-track options and inpatient bed planning?

How the analysis was built

Python (Pandas and NumPy) generated synthetic visits with controlled data-quality issues. Excel Power Query was used for cleaning and validation; Excel supported KPI calculations and exploration; Power BI Desktop provided the dashboard. The raw generator produced 4,060 rows including 60 intentional duplicates for 4,000 unique visits.

Selected findings

Volume peaks in the afternoon

Afternoon arrivals account for 36% of visits; Monday records 660 visits in this synthetic scenario.

Wait target performance

The median provider wait is 50 minutes. Among patients seen by a provider, 32% meet the project’s assumed wait target.

Patients leaving without being seen

218 visits (5.5%) are classified as LWBS. The afternoon LWBS rate is 6.5%, compared with 3.3% at night.

Admission boarding

The median time from admission decision to ED departure is 224 minutes among admitted visits. The reported admission rate is measured among seen visits.

Potential operational responses

  • Evaluate an afternoon fast-track workflow for lower-acuity patients.
  • Review admission bed coordination during afternoon and evening peaks.
  • Monitor waiting times and LWBS patterns together rather than treating them as independent measures.
  • Pilot and evaluate changes before claiming improvements.

These are discussion points generated from simulated patterns, not validated interventions for a real hospital.

Data ethics and limitations

Cedarbridge General Hospital is fictional, and all patient visits are synthetic. No real patient information or clinical outcomes are represented. Wait targets are analytical assumptions, not hospital or clinical standards. The dataset does not include staffing, bed capacity or diagnostic information; the findings are descriptive and do not establish causes.
